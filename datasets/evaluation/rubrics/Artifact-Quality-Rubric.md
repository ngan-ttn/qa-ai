# Artifact Quality Rubric

## Evaluation Metadata

- Evaluation ID: `EVAL-RUBRIC-001`
- Evaluation Type: `Quality Rubric`
- Applies To: `EVAL-CRITERIA-001`
- Scope: `QA-AI Generated Artifacts`
- Status: `Approved`
- Purpose: Define observable quality levels for evaluating QA-AI artifacts against the canonical artifact quality criteria.

---

## Purpose

This document defines the canonical quality rubric used to judge QA artifacts generated by the QA-AI framework.

It translates the quality dimensions defined in:

`datasets/evaluation/criteria/Artifact-Quality-Criteria.md`

into observable evaluation levels.

The rubric answers:

> How well does the generated artifact satisfy each applicable quality criterion?

It does not define:

- Criterion weights
- Numeric aggregation
- Overall pass/fail thresholds
- Benchmark comparison formulas
- Platform ranking rules
- Skill execution behavior

Those responsibilities belong to the scoring and benchmark layers.

---

## Rubric Principles

### Evidence-Based

Every rating must be supported by observable evidence from the evaluation inputs.

A rating must not be based only on writing preference or similarity to a golden output.

### Criterion-Specific

Each criterion must be evaluated independently before an overall result is calculated.

Strong performance in one criterion must not hide a serious weakness in another.

### Artifact-Aware

Only criteria applicable to the artifact type should be evaluated.

The evaluator must respect the applicability defined by the canonical criteria.

### Source-Grounded

The source requirement and authoritative inputs remain the primary truth.

Golden outputs provide reference interpretations but are not exact-match answer keys.

### Severity-Aware

A small clarity issue is not equivalent to:

- Contradicting the requirement
- Fabricating a business rule
- Producing an incorrect expected result
- Omitting critical behavior

Critical failures must be recorded separately from ordinary quality weaknesses.

### Reproducible

Two competent reviewers using the same evidence should be able to reach materially similar ratings.

The evaluator should therefore reference concrete evidence when assigning a level below the highest level.

---

## Rubric Scale

The canonical rubric uses five quality levels.

| Level | Label | General Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| L4 | Excellent | Fully satisfies the criterion with no material quality issue |
| L3 | Good | Satisfies the criterion with minor non-blocking weakness |
| L2 | Partial | Satisfies the criterion only partially; meaningful improvement is required |
| L1 | Poor | Substantial quality problems significantly reduce usefulness or reliability |
| L0 | Failed | Criterion is fundamentally violated or unusable |

The rubric level is a quality classification.

Any numeric value associated with these levels is defined separately by the scoring model.

---

## Rating Rules

### L4 — Excellent

Assign `L4` when:

- The criterion is fully satisfied.
- No material defect is identified.
- Any remaining difference is stylistic or negligible.
- The artifact can be used for its intended purpose without correction related to this criterion.

### L3 — Good

Assign `L3` when:

- The criterion is substantially satisfied.
- One or more minor weaknesses exist.
- The weakness does not materially change business meaning or core coverage.
- Limited cleanup or refinement would improve the artifact.

### L2 — Partial

Assign `L2` when:

- Important parts of the criterion are satisfied but meaningful gaps remain.
- The artifact is still partially usable.
- Correction is required before the artifact should be treated as high quality.
- The issue may affect coverage, precision, traceability, or reviewability.

### L1 — Poor

Assign `L1` when:

- The criterion is weakly satisfied.
- Major defects are present.
- Significant rework is required.
- The artifact may mislead downstream QA activity if used without correction.

### L0 — Failed

Assign `L0` when:

- The criterion is fundamentally violated.
- The artifact contradicts authoritative input, fabricates critical behavior, or otherwise fails the criterion at a foundational level.
- The affected content cannot be trusted without substantial reconstruction.

# Criterion Rubrics

## C01 — Requirement Fidelity

### L4 — Excellent

- All material source behavior is preserved accurately.
- Thresholds, values, states, roles, conditions, and constraints match the source.
- Confirmed and unconfirmed behavior are clearly distinguished.
- No unsupported behavior is presented as required.

### L3 — Good

- Source behavior is materially accurate.
- Minor terminology or framing differences exist.
- No business-critical meaning is changed.
- No significant unsupported behavior is introduced.

### L2 — Partial

- Most core behavior is represented correctly.
- One or more meaningful source details are weakened, omitted, or imprecisely represented.
- The artifact remains recognizable as an interpretation of the source but requires correction.

### L1 — Poor

- Multiple important requirement details are distorted or omitted.
- Source scope is materially misunderstood.
- Unsupported interpretation significantly affects the artifact.

### L0 — Failed

- The artifact contradicts explicit source behavior.
- Critical thresholds, states, roles, or conditions are materially changed.
- Fabricated behavior is presented as authoritative requirement behavior.

---

## C02 — Correctness

### L4 — Excellent

- Derived QA content is logically valid.
- Expected behavior agrees with authoritative input.
- Rules, states, boundaries, risks, and dependencies are reasoned correctly.
- No material internal logic error exists.

### L3 — Good

- Core reasoning is correct.
- Minor logical imprecision exists but does not invalidate the artifact's main conclusions.

### L2 — Partial

- Some derived content is correct while meaningful reasoning defects exist.
- At least one non-critical conclusion, transition, expectation, or relationship requires correction.

### L1 — Poor

- Multiple major logical errors exist.
- Incorrect reasoning significantly affects downstream usability.
- Important expected behavior or derived relationships cannot be trusted.

### L0 — Failed

- Core conclusions are logically invalid.
- Critical expected results contradict the source.
- The artifact is fundamentally unreliable.

---

## C03 — Completeness

### L4 — Excellent

- All relevant source-defined behavior applicable to the artifact is covered.
- Critical states, rules, boundaries, roles, and failure paths are represented where applicable.
- No material coverage gap exists.

### L3 — Good

- Core behavior is fully covered.
- Only minor secondary coverage is missing.
- Missing content does not materially weaken the artifact's primary purpose.

### L2 — Partial

- Major behavior is represented but one or more meaningful coverage gaps remain.
- Additional content is required for dependable QA use.

### L1 — Poor

- Multiple important behaviors are missing.
- Coverage is substantially incomplete.
- Downstream QA activity would likely miss important verification areas.

### L0 — Failed

- Critical source behavior is absent.
- The artifact fails to cover its primary requirement area.
- The artifact cannot fulfill its intended coverage purpose.

---

## C04 — Scope Control

### L4 — Excellent

- Content stays within both source scope and artifact responsibility.
- Confirmed, potential, unknown, and out-of-scope behavior are separated correctly.
- No unjustified downstream or implementation expansion occurs.

### L3 — Good

- Scope is generally controlled.
- Minor unnecessary content exists but does not create false requirements or materially distort artifact responsibility.

### L2 — Partial

- Some scope leakage or responsibility overlap exists.
- Unsupported adjacent content is present but distinguishable from the core valid content.
- Cleanup is required.

### L1 — Poor

- Significant out-of-scope behavior or implementation assumptions are mixed into confirmed content.
- Artifact responsibility is substantially blurred.

### L0 — Failed

- The artifact fundamentally exceeds or misunderstands its scope.
- Unsupported architecture, business behavior, or downstream outputs are presented as confirmed requirements.

---

## C05 — Assumption Control

### L4 — Excellent

- Missing information is explicitly recognized.
- Unknown behavior remains unknown.
- Clarification-dependent content is clearly separated.
- No material unsupported assumption is converted into fact.

### L3 — Good

- Assumptions are generally controlled.
- Minor inferred details appear but do not materially change expected behavior or scope.

### L2 — Partial

- Several assumptions are insufficiently labeled.
- Some unsupported behavior is implied.
- The artifact remains usable after clarification or cleanup.

### L1 — Poor

- Important missing behavior is silently assumed.
- Assumptions materially influence QA conclusions or expected results.

### L0 — Failed

- Critical unsupported assumptions are presented as confirmed truth.
- Fabricated behavior materially defines the artifact.

---

## C06 — Traceability

### L4 — Excellent

- Relevant content is traceable to source behavior and related artifacts.
- IDs and references are stable and correct.
- No confirmed orphan item exists.
- Relationships are easy to review.

### L3 — Good

- Core traceability is intact.
- Minor missing references or identifier inconsistencies exist without losing the underlying relationship.

### L2 — Partial

- Important traceability exists but several links are incomplete or difficult to verify.
- Manual inference is required to reconstruct some relationships.

### L1 — Poor

- Traceability is substantially broken.
- Multiple confirmed items cannot be reliably connected to their sources or downstream artifacts.

### L0 — Failed

- Critical downstream content has no defensible source traceability.
- Identifiers or relationships are fundamentally inconsistent or misleading.

---

## C07 — Clarity

### L4 — Excellent

- Statements are precise and easy to understand.
- Terminology is consistent.
- Structure supports efficient review.
- The intended QA meaning is unambiguous.

### L3 — Good

- Content is clear overall.
- Minor wording, organization, or verbosity issues exist.
- Meaning remains readily understandable.

### L2 — Partial

- Several statements require interpretation.
- Organization or wording reduces review efficiency.
- Some ambiguity is present.

### L1 — Poor

- Significant ambiguity, inconsistency, or excessive complexity obscures QA intent.
- Reviewers may reasonably interpret important content differently.

### L0 — Failed

- The artifact is materially incomprehensible or misleading.
- Core QA intent cannot be determined reliably.

---

## C08 — Testability

### L4 — Excellent

- Applicable behavior is expressed using concrete conditions and observable outcomes.
- Expected behavior is deterministic where the source permits it.
- Verification can be performed without inventing missing requirements.

### L3 — Good

- Most behavior is directly testable.
- Minor details require interpretation but do not prevent meaningful verification.

### L2 — Partial

- Some important behavior is testable while other meaningful portions are vague or insufficiently observable.
- Additional clarification or refinement is needed.

### L1 — Poor

- Major portions rely on subjective or vague outcomes.
- Execution would require substantial tester inference.

### L0 — Failed

- Core behavior cannot be objectively verified from the artifact.
- Expected outcomes are absent, contradictory, or fundamentally non-testable.

---

## C09 — Coverage Efficiency

### L4 — Excellent

- Each item has a distinct and useful primary objective.
- Necessary overlap is purposeful.
- No material redundant coverage exists.
- Coverage is comprehensive without unnecessary expansion.

### L3 — Good

- Coverage is efficient overall.
- Minor redundancy exists but does not materially reduce maintainability.

### L2 — Partial

- Several items overlap unnecessarily.
- Some consolidation or decomposition is required to improve coverage quality.

### L1 — Poor

- Significant duplication obscures actual coverage.
- Many items repeat the same objective or create maintenance burden.

### L0 — Failed

- The artifact is dominated by redundant or poorly decomposed content.
- Effective coverage cannot be reliably distinguished from duplication.

---

## C10 — Boundary and State Coverage

### L4 — Excellent

- All applicable critical boundaries are covered.
- Both sides of thresholds are represented where required.
- Relevant states, transitions, resets, and sequences are correctly handled.

### L3 — Good

- Critical boundaries and states are covered.
- A minor secondary transition or non-critical boundary is missing.

### L2 — Partial

- Some important boundaries or transitions are covered, but meaningful gaps remain.
- Additional boundary or lifecycle coverage is required.

### L1 — Poor

- Critical boundary or state behavior is substantially under-covered.
- Important transitions or sequence dependencies are ignored.

### L0 — Failed

- A source-defined critical boundary or state transition is fundamentally misunderstood or absent.
- The resulting artifact could validate incorrect system behavior.

### Not Applicable

If the source and artifact contain no meaningful boundary, state, transition, or sequence behavior, record the criterion as `N/A`.

`N/A` must not be interpreted as either good or poor quality.

---

## C11 — Risk Awareness

### L4 — Excellent

- Relevant business-critical and failure-prone areas are identified.
- Risk reasoning is specific to the feature.
- Priority reflects credible impact and likelihood considerations where applicable.
- Critical risks receive appropriate QA attention.

### L3 — Good

- Major risks are recognized.
- Minor prioritization or reasoning detail could be improved.
- No critical risk is obviously ignored.

### L2 — Partial

- Some meaningful risks are identified, but important failure modes or prioritization considerations are missing.

### L1 — Poor

- Risk analysis is generic, shallow, or materially incomplete.
- Critical failure modes receive insufficient attention.

### L0 — Failed

- Critical risk is ignored or materially misrepresented.
- Risk reasoning would direct QA effort away from known high-impact behavior.

### Not Applicable

For artifact types where risk awareness is only supporting and no risk decision is required, the evaluator may record `N/A` when justified by the evaluation profile.

---

## C12 — Internal Consistency

### L4 — Excellent

- Terminology, values, IDs, states, rules, and relationships are internally consistent.
- Related artifact expectations agree.
- No material contradiction exists.

### L3 — Good

- Overall consistency is strong.
- Minor naming or reference inconsistency exists without affecting meaning.

### L2 — Partial

- Several inconsistencies exist.
- Reviewers can still determine the intended behavior, but correction is required.

### L1 — Poor

- Significant conflicts exist across sections or related artifacts.
- QA conclusions become unreliable without substantial reconciliation.

### L0 — Failed

- Core values, rules, states, or expectations directly conflict.
- The artifact cannot be trusted as a coherent QA reference.

# Critical Failure Handling

The following defects require explicit critical-failure recording when supported by the evaluation evidence.

## CF-01 — Source Contradiction

The generated artifact directly contradicts explicit authoritative source behavior.

Typical affected criteria:

- C01
- C02
- C12

## CF-02 — Fabricated Business Behavior

Unsupported behavior is presented as confirmed business logic.

Typical affected criteria:

- C01
- C04
- C05

## CF-03 — Critical Coverage Omission

A business-critical source behavior applicable to the artifact is missing.

Typical affected criteria:

- C03
- C06
- C10

## CF-04 — Incorrect Critical Expected Result

An executable artifact defines a critical expected result that conflicts with authoritative behavior.

Typical affected criteria:

- C01
- C02
- C08

## CF-05 — Broken Critical Traceability

Critical downstream content cannot be connected to a defensible source.

Typical affected criteria:

- C06
- C12

## CF-06 — Unsupported Scope Expansion

Unsupported architecture, API, database, role, dependency, or other behavior is represented as confirmed.

Typical affected criteria:

- C04
- C05

## CF-07 — Internal Critical Conflict

Two material parts of the evaluated artifact or artifact chain define incompatible behavior.

Typical affected criteria:

- C02
- C12

A critical failure does not automatically define the final numeric score in this rubric.

The scoring model determines:

- Score caps
- Penalties
- Overall result impact
- Pass/fail consequences

# Evidence Requirements

## L4 Evidence

An `L4` rating requires sufficient review evidence to conclude that no material issue exists for the applicable criterion.

Absence of an observed defect alone is not enough when the evaluator has not reviewed the relevant source evidence.

## L3–L0 Evidence

Any rating below `L4` should record:

- Criterion ID
- Observed issue
- Supporting evidence
- Affected artifact section or item
- Why the issue corresponds to the assigned level

Where practical, also record:

- Recommended correction
- Related critical failure ID, if applicable

---

# Handling Not Applicable Criteria

A criterion may be marked `N/A` only when:

1. The criterion is genuinely irrelevant to the artifact and evaluation profile.
2. The reason is documented.
3. The criterion is not being skipped merely because evaluation is difficult.
4. The applicable criteria definition permits contextual applicability.

Examples:

- Boundary and State Coverage may be `N/A` when no boundary, state, transition, or sequence behavior exists.
- Risk Awareness may be `N/A` for an artifact profile where risk reasoning is not required.

Core criteria such as Requirement Fidelity and Correctness should not normally be marked `N/A` for generated QA artifacts.

The scoring model defines how `N/A` criteria affect aggregation.

# Artifact-Level Interpretation

The same rubric level may manifest differently across artifact types.

## Requirement Analysis

High-quality performance emphasizes:

- Accurate feature understanding
- Scope separation
- Missing-information detection
- User-flow and behavior completeness
- No premature downstream invention

## Business Rules

High-quality performance emphasizes:

- Atomic rules
- Correct conditions and outcomes
- Stable identifiers
- Source traceability
- No invented rules

## Risk Analysis

High-quality performance emphasizes:

- Feature-specific risks
- Credible impact reasoning
- Appropriate priority
- Coverage relevance
- Separation of confirmed and implementation-dependent risks

## Test Scenarios

High-quality performance emphasizes:

- Distinct scenario objectives
- Requirement coverage
- Positive and negative behavior
- Boundary and state coverage
- Clarification-dependent separation

## Test Cases

High-quality performance emphasizes:

- Executability
- Atomic objectives
- Deterministic expected results
- Appropriate test data
- Traceability
- No invented implementation validation

## Regression Analysis

High-quality performance emphasizes:

- Evidence-based impact identification
- Direct-versus-potential impact separation
- Regression prioritization
- Reuse of confirmed test coverage
- Dependency and assumption control

Artifact-specific expectations refine the interpretation of the criteria.

They do not replace the canonical criterion definitions.

# Evaluation Procedure

For each generated artifact:

1. Identify the artifact type.
2. Identify the authoritative evaluation inputs.
3. Determine applicable criteria.
4. Review the artifact against each applicable criterion independently.
5. Assign `L4`, `L3`, `L2`, `L1`, or `L0`.
6. Record evidence for any rating below `L4`.
7. Record any applicable critical failure.
8. Mark genuinely irrelevant criteria as `N/A` with justification.
9. Pass the rubric result to the scoring layer.
10. Preserve the evaluation record for benchmark or regression comparison when applicable.

# Rubric Boundaries

This rubric must not be used to:

- Require exact wording from golden outputs.
- Penalize harmless formatting differences.
- Reward unsupported content merely because it appears comprehensive.
- Infer missing system behavior.
- Introduce architecture assumptions.
- Convert stylistic preference into a correctness defect.
- Assign final weighted scores without the scoring model.
- Compare platforms without the benchmark rules.

# Relationship to Evaluation Architecture

The canonical evaluation flow is:

`Evaluation Inputs → Criteria → Rubric → Scoring → Benchmark / Regression Comparison`

## Criteria

Defines what quality dimensions matter.

## Rubric

Defines observable quality levels.

This document belongs to this layer.

## Scoring

Converts rubric results into measurable evaluation outcomes.

## Benchmark

Uses comparable evaluation outcomes to establish baselines, compare platforms, or detect regressions.

---

# Final Rubric Definition

The canonical QA-AI artifact quality rubric consists of:

- `12` criterion-level rubrics corresponding to `C01–C12`
- `5` quality levels: `L4`, `L3`, `L2`, `L1`, `L0`
- Explicit `N/A` handling where applicable
- `7` critical failure categories
- Evidence requirements for non-excellent ratings
- Artifact-aware interpretation guidance

The rubric provides the qualitative judgment layer between the canonical evaluation criteria and the scoring model.
